Duskline

An original anime JRPG in the spirit of Final Fantasy, Xenoblade and Persona. Turn-based battles you can play with one thumb, ten-minute roguelite runs, and a gacha that belongs to the story. Built by one developer in TypeScript, for the web first and then iOS and Android.

  • Solo developer
  • TypeScript · PixiJS · Spine
  • Web → iOS / Android
  • Turn-based, portrait-first
  • 10–15 min runs
  • Earned-currency gacha first

Research current to September 2026. Prices, laws and platform rules change, so re-check the linked sources before you spend money.

Summary

The short version

You're one developer who knows TypeScript, so the plan is built around that. It uses a small, reusable art pipeline and systems that create replay without constant new story. The gacha starts on earned currency, so you can learn what works before money or regulation is involved.

Go 2D, illustrated and rigged

Painted splash art, Spine-rigged battle sprites and a crisp HTML/SVG interface give a sharp anime look for far less than 3D costs, and they fit a phone browser's memory.

One-thumb combat with real depth

Turn-based with the turn order always visible. Hit weaknesses to Break enemies, chain follow-ups, then finish with a party-wide Horizon Burst. Tap a skill, then a target.

Replay from ten-minute Descents

A roguelite run on a daily seed shared by all players gives short, varied sessions without needing a new story chapter every week.

Earned-currency gacha first

Real-money gacha brings odds rules, age checks and a content treadmill. Launch with earned pulls and direct sales, and decide on paid pulls using beta data.

Web first, stores second

A link is the easiest playtest there is. The same TypeScript build becomes iOS and Android apps through Capacitor once the game proves itself.

About 28 months to 1.0

That assumes roughly 15 hours a week. Each phase ends with a gate you can measure and has a list of what to cut if it runs late.

Proof that the pieces work on phones and the web

Honkai: Star Rail

A turn-based gacha JRPG built for phones. Enemies carry a Toughness bar that only weakness hits deplete, and its Simulated Universe is a roguelike mode where you pick one of three blessings after each fight.

Persona 5: The Phantom X

Persona-style combat and menus as a free-to-play gacha on iOS, Android and PC. Built in Unity, it launched worldwide on June 26, 2025.

Granblue Fantasy

A gacha JRPG that has run in web browsers and on phones since 2014. It reunited art director Hideo Minaba and composer Nobuo Uematsu from Final Fantasy V, VI and IX.

Another Eden

A long-running gacha JRPG with no stamina system, written by Masato Kato with music by Yasunori Mitsuda. In September 2026 it was rebuilt as Another Eden Begins, a gacha-free premium game.

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33

A turn-based RPG with timed parries that won Game of the Year at The Game Awards 2025. Turn-based combat is firmly back in the mainstream.

Arknights

Adding the Integrated Strategies roguelike mode raised average playtime and made long-retired units worth using again.

Warning

Square Enix has closed a string of Final Fantasy gacha games. Record Keeper closed globally in 2022, Dissidia Opera Omnia in 2024 and War of the Visions in 2025–26, and Final Fantasy VII Ever Crisis ends service on October 6, 2026. Its producer pointed to the cost of producing enough new weapons and costumes to keep the gacha economy going. A one-person game has to be designed so it never depends on that pace.

Art

Art direction

Across Xenoblade, Persona, Final Fantasy and newer anime games, the sharp look comes more from strong illustration and graphic design than from rendering power. That suits a solo web game.

Xenoblade Chronicles 2 and 3

Masatsugu Saito led character design on XC2, Torna, XC3 and Future Redeemed. For XC3, the team made the cast taller and more mature with quieter costumes to suit a serious story, and kept his illustrations intact without a figurine-like or CG look. XC2's rare Blades came from a long list of guest artists, including Tetsuya Nomura and Kunihiko Tanaka.

TakeThe illustration is the source of truth. Guest-designed units are a proven gacha hook.

Persona 3, 4 and 5

Masayoshi Suto designed Atlus's interfaces from Nocturne through Persona 5. Each game has one main color: teal, then a vivid yellow, then crimson red with only black and white. Persona 5 uses ransom-note lettering to fit its rebel theme, and Shigenori Soejima dresses the cast in contemporary fashion.

TakeOne signature color and kinetic type give an instant identity at low cost.

Metaphor: ReFantazio

Lead UI designer Koji Ise came to Atlus from website design, and Metaphor was his first game. The UI team was asked to help set the game's whole art direction.

TakeYour web skills carry over. Build the menus in HTML, CSS and SVG.

Final Fantasy

Yoshitaka Amano's watercolor, ukiyo-e-influenced art shaped the logos and early casts. Tetsuya Nomura's stylized Final Fantasy VII designs followed, and Akihiko Yoshida keeps character colors consistent with their environments.

TakeA painted logo and key art carry the brand. Tie every character's colors to the world's light.

Guilty Gear Xrd

Arc System Works' GDC 2015 talk shows 3D characters built to read as 2D anime, with hand-tuned shading and animation that holds poses on limited frames.

TakeHeld poses, smears and impact frames make attacks feel like anime in any renderer.

Granblue Fantasy

Hideo Minaba's illustrated 2D art has carried a browser and mobile gacha JRPG since 2014.

TakeIllustration-led 2D works on the web at gacha scale.

Zenless Zone Zero

Its lead designer comes from packaging and graphic design, and the city is built from signage, brands and graffiti.

TakeGraphic design is cheap, distinctive world-building.

Octopath Traveler

HD-2D puts pixel characters in 3D dioramas with dynamic light, depth of field and tilt-shift.

TakeA solid option for one developer, though it reads as retro rather than sharp anime.

Proportions
Seven to seven and a half heads tall for story art, like Xenoblade 3's matured cast. Battle sprites use the same design. Chibi tokens appear only on the world map.
Line and shade
Clean tapered line art, colored a darker shade of the fill instead of pure black. Two-tone cel shading plus one hard highlight, with no airbrush.
Light
Rim light takes the sky color of the scene: gold on the Blaze side, rose on the Duskline, teal in the Hush. Characters always sit in the world's light.
Silhouette
Each character has one shape that reads at 48 pixels, such as hair, a hat or a weapon. Test it by filling the sprite solid black.
Color
Three main colors and one accent per character, with faction coding: white and gold for the Dominion, rose and amber for the Strider cities, indigo and teal for the Hushborn.
Motion
Anime timing for attacks: held key poses, two- or three-frame smears and one-frame impact flashes. Smooth 60 fps only for idle breathing and the interface.
Interface
A 12-degree horizon diagonal cuts every panel. Dusk Rose, ink and paper only. Heavy display type for battle callouts. Menus in HTML, CSS and SVG.
Backgrounds
Painted scenes in four to six parallax layers under a live gradient sky, with giant silhouettes such as walking cities and the Anchor on the horizon for scale.
Sharpness
Render at the device's pixel ratio, capped at 2×. Pack textures at twice their display size, use SVG icons and DOM or MSDF text, and never scale bitmap UI.

Lore

World and lore starter

A thousand years ago the world of Hesper stopped turning. One half burns under a noon that never ends and the other freezes under endless night. People survive on the Duskline, a ring of permanent sunset where cities on colossal legs keep walking to stay in the light.

The Blaze (noonward)

Bleached deserts and mirrored glass cities under a sun that never moves. Home of the Meridian Dominion.

White, gold, hard shadows

The Duskline

The inhabited ring. Walking cities called Striders, lantern markets, wind farms and caravans. Your home is Vesper, the oldest and smallest Strider.

Rose, amber, violet

The Hush (nightward)

Frozen seas under auroras, glowing forests, and the Umbral Reach, where memory storms remake the land every night.

Indigo, teal, starlight

Factions

The Meridian Dominion

Holds the Anchor, a pillar at the noon pole said to keep the world still. It teaches that the Stillness was a mercy, and mines Gloamstones to run its sun-engines.

The Strider Compact

A loose alliance of walking cities: traders, salvagers, and the Lamplighters who can kindle Gloamstones.

The Hushborn

People of the night side. They keep the old calendars and treat Gloamstones as graves.

The Fades

The enemies. Memories that lost their person, pouring out of the Hush during memory storms as paper-cut silhouettes with static at the edges.

The mystery

Gloamstones hold people from earlier Turnings, ages of the world that each ended when Hesper completed a rotation. The Dominion's founders stopped the rotation to end the cycle of erasure. The Stillness is slowly killing the world anyway, because the Duskline narrows every year. Io, the first Afterlight you kindle, remembers the very first Turning, and remembers the world moving. The story asks whether to restart the Turning, keep the Stillness, or find a third way.

The themes are memory and letting go, the cost of standing still, and a found family choosing its own future. They echo Xenoblade's cycles, Final Fantasy's rebels against an empire, and Persona's question of who you are underneath.

Story shape

Launch with a prologue and three chapters, about 6–8 hours with side content. Each chapter is 60–90 minutes of story in one region, with one Descent biome, two bond arcs and a boss. After launch, add a chapter or story event every 6–8 weeks.

Two other directions, if Hesper isn't the one

Midnight Transit

Leans toward Persona. The last train of the night never stops at the terminal. It runs on into the Undercity, a subway network built from the city's unfinished dreams. The gacha is the passengers whose dreams you rescue, and each night's route is different.

Crownless Sky

Leans toward Final Fantasy. Seven moons hold up a cracked sky. When one fell, its shards became the Starbound, people reborn with a god's fragment, and an empire now hunts them to rebuild the Crown. Moon dungeons reshape with the lunar phase.

Before you announce

Run a trademark search on the working title. Avoid series-owned terms such as Monado, Blade, Persona, Materia and Aeon, and keep character designs clear of famous look-alikes.

Design

Game design

Pillars

One thumb, real decisions

Battles and menus work one-handed in portrait. The depth comes from choices, not from complicated inputs.

Ten minutes is a full session

Every mode has a satisfying place to stop within 10–15 minutes.

Bonds make pulls matter

Every Afterlight has scenes and a reason to exist in the story, so a pull is about a person as much as their stats.

Every screen is sunset

The twilight palette, the horizon diagonal and giant silhouettes appear on every screen.

Combat: Break and Link

One chain of five steps. Each step borrows from a game that proved it works.

  1. Hit a weakness

    Weakness hits crack the enemy's Shell and earn an Encore.

    Persona 5 · Honkai: Star Rail

  2. Encore or Pass

    Take one extra action, or pass it to an ally who hasn't acted. Each pass adds a little damage.

    Persona 5's One More and Baton Pass

  3. Break

    At zero Shell the enemy loses its next turn and takes 50% more damage.

    FFVII Remake's stagger · Star Rail's Weakness Break

  4. Topple, then Launch

    Follow-up skills extend the chain for more delay and a guaranteed critical hit.

    Xenoblade 3's combos

  5. Horizon Burst

    When every enemy is Broken, the whole party strikes in one cut-in.

    Persona's All-Out Attack · Xenoblade's Chain Attack

  • A party of four. The turn order is always on screen, and previewing a skill shows how it shifts, as in Final Fantasy X's CTB.
  • Six affinities: Sun, Moon, Flame, Frost, Gale and Volt. Each has its own icon shape and label, so weakness never depends on color alone.
  • Ultimates charge as you act and take hits. Tap a portrait to fire one between turns, as in Honkai: Star Rail.
  • An optional guard tap as a hit lands cuts its damage by 30%. It's off in auto mode and never required. Clair Obscur made timing inputs popular again, and here they stay a bonus.
  • Auto-battle and 2× speed unlock once a stage is cleared, and cleared stages can be swept.

The Descent

  • Three floors of four nodes each, ending in a floor boss. Nodes are battles, elites, events, rests, shops and Echoes.
  • After each battle, pick one of three Glimmers (boons). Glimmers belong to Paths, so each run builds toward a style, like Honkai: Star Rail's Simulated Universe.
  • Echo nodes lend you an Afterlight you don't own for the rest of the run. Players try units before pulling, and old units stay useful, as in Arknights' Integrated Strategies.
  • Everyone plays the same daily seed. Because the battle core is deterministic, the server can replay a submitted run to check its score.
  • A failed run still pays part of its rewards. Permanent upgrades are small and capped, so skill matters more than grinding.
  • Gloam from Descents has a weekly cap, which prevents burnout.

Bonds

Each character has five bond ranks. A rank is a 1–3 minute scene written in ink that reuses existing portraits and backgrounds. Ranks unlock a small passive, a lore entry and, at rank 5, a new outfit color. Bond scenes are the cheapest content you can make per minute of play, and they carry much of the emotional weight.

World map

Each region is a node map over a painted vista, with one hero illustration reused as its chapter title card. It gives Xenoblade's sense of scale without an open world.

Gacha

Gacha design and business model

The gacha is called Kindling. You spend Gloam, a currency earned in play, to kindle Gloamstones and call Afterlights. The business model is still open, so this section recommends a fair default, lets you test the numbers, and compares four ways to make money.

What's in the pool

  • Afterlights (★5 and ★4). Playable characters. They cost the most to make, so there are few of them.
  • Memory Cards (★5, ★4, ★3). Equippable illustrations with a passive effect, like Fate/Grand Order's Craft Essences. Each is a single illustration, so they're cheap to add.
  • Alternate memories. Other-Turning versions of existing characters, with a new outfit, palette and kit on an existing rig.

Recommended starting rules

  • ★5 base rate of 2%. Soft pity starts at pull 45 and adds 6.5% per pull, and pull 60 is guaranteed.
  • ★4 rate of 12%, with at least one ★4 in every 10 pulls.
  • 60% of ★5 results are the featured Afterlight. After a miss, the next ★5 is guaranteed to be featured.
  • A spark at 120 pulls on a banner lets you pick the featured unit. At about 20 free pulls a week that's six weeks, one full cycle.
  • Pity carries over between banners. Unused spark points turn into Gloam when a banner ends.
  • Duplicates raise Resonance from rank 1 to 5, then convert to Gloam.
  • Odds and each player's pull history are always visible in the game.
  • No limited-only units in the first year. Every featured unit reruns within about three months.
  • No rewards for completing a set of random items. Japan banned that mechanic, kompu gacha, in 2012.

Recommendation. Launch free with earned-only pulls and sell things directly: a supporter pack, outfits, and a season pass with fixed rewards. With no paid randomness, the loot-box rules below don't apply, and players keep trusting you while you learn. Build the pull service on the server with odds tables and an audit log from day one, so paid pulls can be added later without a rebuild.

Revisit the choice at the end of the open beta. Paid pulls only make sense if day-7 retention is 8% or higher, you can pay for a legal review, and you can ship a new unit every cycle or have a publisher who can. Another Eden shows both paths are real: it ran for years as a gacha, then came back as a gacha-free premium release for players who dislike gacha.

Paying on the web

In the US, apps can now link to web checkout. Apple has charged no commission on linked purchases since a 2025 contempt ruling, and the Supreme Court takes up the fight in its October 2026 term. Performance budgets for phones

First load
Title screen interactive within 3 seconds on 4G, with 5 MB or less downloaded.
First battle
25 MB or less in total. Stream everything else by region.
Frame rate
60 fps on a three- to four-year-old mid-range Android phone, and never below 30. Cap rendering at 2× device pixels.
Memory
Keep texture memory around 300 MB or less, pack atlases at 2048 px, and unload regions the player leaves.
Battle scene
At most eight skeletons on screen, with one or two atlas pages per character.
Saves
Local first in IndexedDB, synced to the cloud, with a versioned save format.

Engineering habits

  • Keep the battle core deterministic: the same seed and inputs always give the same result. The server can then verify Descent scores, and the simulator can balance units.
  • Keep content in data files checked by schemas such as zod, never in code.
  • Run 10,000 simulated battles in CI for every balance change.
  • Version the save format from day one and write migrations.
  • Keep pulls, currency and rewards on the server. The client only asks.
  • Ship accessibility options: text size, reduced motion, affinity icons that don't depend on color, and hold-to-confirm for spending.

With a commissioned splash, it's about 55–95 hours. At 15 hours a week, one new Afterlight takes 4–8 weeks of all your time. So plan on a launch roster of 12, then at most one new Afterlight per 6–8-week cycle. Fill banners with alternate memories, Memory Cards and reruns.

Art sourcing and AI

Commission character art from human artists under contracts that grant full commercial rights. The reasons are practical.

  • The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report says prompts alone don't make you the author, so purely AI-generated art may not be protected. In a game whose product is its characters, that's a serious gap.
  • Steam requires disclosure of AI-generated content that players see, under rules rewritten in January 2026.
  • Anime gacha players study splash art closely, and finding AI art in a reveal can sink trust overnight.

Music and tools

Commission the main theme and battle theme during the vertical slice, because they define the game as much as the art. Make sure the license covers streaming and soundtrack sales. For tools, use Clip Studio Paint or Krita for illustration, Spine for rigs, Figma for UI, Inky for writing and Blender for background blockouts.
